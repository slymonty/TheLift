# THE LIFT — Game Bible v0.5

> **The Lift** carries four meanings: the elevator you're climbing, carrying a downed teammate, the criminal sense of *lifting* something that isn't yours, and the bird that takes four people off the roof.

**Genre:** Melee extraction — grounded, close-quarters, desperate
**Modes:** 8–12 FFA · 2v2v2 · **3v3v3** · 4v4v4
**Match length:** 14–20 minutes
**Perspective:** Third person, over-shoulder, soft lock-on

### Changes from v0.4
- **Premise rewritten again.** Retrieval crews → **people in debt.** The casting problem is solved.
- **Genre register named:** the botched job, not the heist.
- **Debt as progression** — replaces cosmetics-only. Solves the grind question.
- **Hold-or-take** — the alert fires on *pickup*, not discovery. Finding an asset and leaving it is a strategy.
- **Dead air timer** — prevents indefinite camping.
- **The alert comes from your own creditor**, not a neutral contractor. The leak is on your side of the phone.
- **Untrained body language** promoted to a hard art constraint.
- Two-hand system, throwing tables, and crew roster added.

# PART I — THE PITCH

## One line

Nine people who owe money get sent into a collapsing tower to steal the same thing. Four seats leave at dawn.

## Who these people are

**Not thieves. Not professionals. Collateral.**

A guy who owes a bookie. A woman whose brother owes a bookie. A dockworker caught skimming. A nurse in debt to the wrong clinic. Ordinary people with ordinary jobs and one bad arrangement.

The people they owe have a standing offer: **go in, bring it out, your debt is cleared.** Refuse and it isn't.

That's the whole casting. Professionals don't take a job with a four-in-nine survival rate. **Desperate amateurs do.**

## Why three crews

**Three different creditors want the same thing**, and none of them trust each other enough to cooperate. So each sends their own collateral.

The people in the building have no personal stake in the item at all. Most of them don't know what it is. They want to stop owing, and everything else is somebody else's business.

## Synopsis

The Verge is a condemned district — six mega-towers left standing after the ground gave out. Cordoned, scheduled for demolition, and full of things people want back.

You were told a floor range and a description. You were not told there'd be company.

**The way in is one-way.** The lower levels flooded and the stairs below three are gone. You come up through a breach and there is no walking out. The only exit is the roof, and **the roof door doesn't open without both halves of the mechanism.**

**One bird comes at dawn.** A contractor with a helicopter who sells seats to whoever's standing on the pad. He doesn't care who. He doesn't wait, and he doesn't come back, because the cordon sweep starts at first light.

**Nine go in. Four come out.** Everybody knew the number.

**Nobody carries a gun.** A shot brings the sweep early and the airspace closes on everyone — not a rule anyone enforces, just arithmetic everyone can do. So it's whatever the building gives you: pipes, fire axes, chairs, extinguishers, a desk lamp.

And the building is coming apart while you're in it.

## The genre register — the botched job

**This is not a heist movie.** Heist movies are about competence: the plan, the crew, the clever execution.

This is the **botched job** — ordinary people, one bad decision, escalating consequences. *Reservoir Dogs. A Simple Plan. Good Time.* The plan goes wrong in the first ten minutes and everything after is amateurs improvising.

That's the register for every line of dialogue, every animation, and every system in this document.

## Why they fight — the friction

**The prize is indivisible.** Loot splits. Floors split. **A seat does not.** More than four people want out, and everyone can do that math on the ground floor.

**The exit requires both halves.** You can't avoid each other. Every route ends at the same door.

**Nobody can enforce a deal.** Different creditors, no law, no repeat game. A truce is worth exactly as long as it's convenient.

**Betrayal has a deadline.** You can honestly cooperate for nine floors and lose nothing, because the fight only *has* to happen on the roof. **Every alliance is real and temporary, and everyone knows it.**

**Delay is as good as violence.** You don't have to beat someone. You have to make them slower than you. Every mechanic here — Cling, Snag, pull-downs, broken legs, traps — is a delay mechanic, and all of them are rational.

**And these people know each other.** It's a small world of the indebted. You've seen the guy in the stairwell at the same back rooms. That's why the fights are personal.

## The six pillars

**1. Every action costs.** One resource — stamina — pays for striking, blocking, sprinting, climbing, carrying, searching, and forcing doors.

**2. Bodies are the currency of progress.** Doors need three people. Ledges need two. Losing a teammate is a *capability* loss that may close a route entirely.

**3. The building does the killing.** Fists chip. Furniture ends people. Hazards don't pick sides.

**4. Nobody is ever fully out of the fight.** Broken players hold doors. Dazed players grab legs. Downed players cling to ankles. **The game never has a passenger.**

**5. Losing a fight is a decision, not an ending.** Carry, revive, or leave — all three legitimate, all three built to hurt.

**6. Delay beats damage.** The win condition is arrival, not elimination.

## The moments we're designing for

*You find it on floor four and you don't pick it up. Nobody knows. The clock hasn't started. You go hunt the other half and leave a man watching the room.*

*Two crews, one half each, standing in a stairwell with the drop prompt up. Neither gets out without the other. Six people, four seats. They trade anyway.*

*A dying man grabs your Heavy's ankle in a doorway. Your Medic turns back to kick him off. Your third is watching the stairs alone. That's when the third crew arrives.*

*One of you backed into the server room and made three of them come through the door one at a time.*

*Dawn comes. The door never opened. Nobody gets out.*

## What this is not

- **Not arcade.** No combos, no move names, no health bar draining in six seconds.
- **Not a wrestling game.** Grapple *architecture* borrowed; vocabulary is not.
- **Not a battle royale.** No circle, no 100 players, no seasonal treadmill.
- **Not a hero shooter.** Archetypes are bodies, not ability kits.
- **Not a martial arts game.** **Nobody is trained. That is the entire point.**
- **Not simulationist.** Grounded, not accurate. Rooms are bigger than real rooms because the camera needs them to be.

# PART II — TONE, THEME & ART

## Theme

**What you're willing to leave behind to get out from under it.**

Secondary: **exhaustion is the real enemy.** The other crews are a problem. The building is a problem. The thing that beats you is being out of gas on floor nine.

## The item

**Never explained.** Nobody in the building knows what it is or why three separate people want it badly enough to get someone killed for it. Nobody asks.

This costs zero writing, keeps focus on the people rather than the MacGuffin, and is more unsettling than any explanation would be.

## References

Def Jam: Fight for NY (weight, environmental violence) · Hunt: Showdown (extraction dread) · The Raid (vertical escalation) · Arc Raiders (environment as third party) · Lethal Company (the social tragedy of abandonment) · Sifu / For Honor (stamina as spine) · WWF No Mercy ladder matches (the climb-or-stomp window) · **A Simple Plan / Good Time (amateurs, escalating)**

## Art direction

**Grounded proportions, PS2-era geometry, 2026 lighting.**

- **Real human silhouettes, real body types.** Heavy people are actually heavy, not muscular-heavy. Nobody is a superhero.
- **Wardrobe is practical and worn.** Work boots, layers, hoodies, coveralls, taped hands, cargo pockets. These are people dressed for a bad night, not a gym or an office.
- **Not cartoon, not stylized-friendly.** If it reads like Fortnite, it's wrong.
- **Lighting is the entire budget.** Firelight, emergency strobes, dust shafts, water caustics, sodium work lamps.
- **Palette:** Concrete grey, rust, sodium orange, emergency red. Colour comes from the disaster, the crew tape, and blood.

**Crew identification is mandatory and decided before any character art.** Three crews in a dark building in grounded clothing is a recognition nightmare. **Crew colour on one loud element** — forearm tape, bandana, a paint stripe across the back. Readable in silhouette, readable in the dark.

**Rule:** if it reads in silhouette at 15m in low light, it ships.

## Untrained body language — hard constraint

*Promoted from a v0.4 note. This governs every animation decision.*

**If a move looks practiced, it's wrong.**

- **Strikes are wide and looping.** Off-balance follow-through. No crisp technique.
- **Recovery looks like a stumble, not a reset.**
- **Grapples are "grab and hurl," not executed throws.** No form. No setup. No control.
- **No held poses. No showboating. No flourishes.**
- **Competence feels accidental.** A clean slip is the *player* getting it right, not the character being skilled.

**Filtering criterion for bought animation packs:** most commercial packs are choreographed to look *skilled*, because that's what most games want. Reject any clip that reads as trained. Prefer MMA/judo/street-brawl packs over pro-wrestling or martial-arts sets, and cut anything with a wind-up flourish or a hold.

## Audio

Audio is a **primary mechanic**, and in Act I it is the *only* mechanic.

- Footsteps loud, surface-dependent, directional.
- **Searching is noisy.** Rummaging desks, prying cabinets. **You hear which floor every crew is on.**
- **Breathing is the stamina bar.** You can hear that an enemy is gassed — which tells you an environmental throw is available.
- **The asset is noisy.** The case knocks against things.
- Impacts dry, heavy, unpleasant. No cartoon whoosh.
- The building has a voice — groans, settling, distant collapse — escalating through the match.
- **Proximity voice by default.** Negotiating a truce you're all going to break is a feature.

# PART III — MATCH STRUCTURE

## 3.1 — Two acts

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  ACT I — THE SEARCH            ~3–6 min                 │
│  Randomized starts. Randomized asset locations.         │
│  Everyone spread out, searching, listening.             │
│  Fights are accidental, not planned.                    │
└────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────┘
                         │
                  ═══ THE ALERT ═══
                  Fires on PICKUP, not discovery.
                         │
┌────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────┐
│  ACT II — THE CLIMB            ~8–14 min                │
│  Carriers are hunted. The building empties upward.      │
│  Assets change hands. Disaster escalates.               │
└────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────┘
                         │
┌────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────┐
│  THE ROOF                      60–90 sec                │
│  Door opens. Four seats. Dawn is coming.                │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## 3.2 — Act I: The Search

**Every crew starts at a randomized entry point.** Different floors, different corners. Nobody starts adjacent.

**Asset locations randomized** within a floor band (typically 2–6). Filing cabinets, safes, desk drawers, server cages, lockers.

### Searching

| Property | Value |
|---|---|
| Time per container | 3–4 seconds |
| Vulnerability | Weapon down, back turned, **blindside eligible (2.2×)** |
| Noise | Loud, directional, floor-identifiable |
| Stamina | 5 flat per container |

**Three rules make the search phase good rather than boring:**

1. **Searching is audible.** You hear which floor every crew is on. A listening game, not a solitary one.
2. **Searching is costly.** Every container is a small gamble with your back turned.
3. **Searching is social.** Proximity voice, three crews, nobody has an asset yet. Everyone knows the fighting is coming and nobody wants to start it early.

## 3.3 — Hold or take

*The most important addition in v0.5.*

**The alert fires on pickup, not on discovery.**

You can find an asset and **leave it where it is.** You know exactly where it is. Nobody else does. The clock hasn't started.

**What that opens up:**

| Play | Logic |
|---|---|
| **Leave and hunt** | Go find the other half while the building is still quiet. Come back for both. |
| **Bait** | Post a man nearby. Anyone who wanders in is walking into an ambush at a spot you chose. |
| **Bank it** | Hold the knowledge as insurance. If the other crews find the other half first, you still have leverage. |
| **Take it now** | Speed over secrecy. You're first up, but every crew in the building knows the floor. |

**The counter-pressure:** the longer you leave it, the more likely somebody else stumbles onto it. Genuine risk on both sides of the decision.

### The dead air timer

**If neither asset is picked up within ~4 minutes of the first being discovered**, the creditors get impatient and broadcast a general location anyway.

Prevents indefinite camping and keeps dawn meaningful. Presented as an irritated radio call, not a UI event.

## 3.4 — The alert

**It comes from your own creditor.**

Three crews, three different creditors, all monitoring the building. When someone lifts an asset, *your* guy tells you the competition moved.

Which means: **the leak is on your side of the phone.** Somebody your creditor talks to also talks to theirs. You are not the client here, you're the collateral, and the people who sent you are trading information about you.

**What's broadcast: the floor number. Nothing else.**

- Just "found" → paranoia with no direction; players mill
- **Found + floor → a direction, not a target. Correct.**
- Found + live position → a straight hunt, kills the tension

**Then periodic pulses** — rough position every 20 seconds, tightening as the carrier ascends. They're being tracked, imperfectly, **and they know it.**

Presentation: a radio crackle. Diegetic, never a UI banner.

## 3.5 — The assets

| Mode | Players | Assets |
|---|---|---|
| **FFA — The Runner** | 8–12 | **1** |
| **Duos** | 2v2v2 (6) | **2** |
| **Trios** | 3v3v3 (9) | **2** — *flagship* |
| **Squads** | 4v4v4 (12) | **2** |

### Two assets = war, not a hunt

**One asset creates a hunt** — a carrier and a mob, all incentives aligned one direction.

**Two assets create a war.** Every crew is simultaneously predator and prey. You can't turtle, because holding your half doesn't get you out. You can't purely aggress, because leaving your half undefended loses it. **No passive strategy exists.**

### The halves

**Two halves of one mechanism** — a key and a cylinder, a card and a codebook. Not two independent keys.

**Why halves:** two separate exits means two crews leave and never meet. One door requiring both means **everyone converges at the top, guaranteed.**

**Neither half is useful alone.** A crew holding one holds a chip they cannot spend, and knows exactly what they're missing and roughly where it is.

**Never three assets.** One crew could be locked out by geometry rather than play, and the endgame becomes an illegible twelve-person pile. **Two at every team count.** The asymmetry is the point — the crew with nothing is the most dangerous one in the building.

### The physical object

**A hardened case, roughly briefcase-sized.**

- **Occupies a hand** — costs a weapon slot
- **Changes the silhouette at distance** — carriers are visually identifiable
- **Readable to a spectator** — "who has the case" is instantly legible
- **Tumbles when dropped** — visible on the floor of a dark room
- **Noisy** — knocks against things while carried

**In FFA the silhouette change is subtle** — tucked under a coat. Tell up close, not across a room. The alert says *someone* has it and the lobby starts reading body language.

### Handoff rules

**The asset drops when the carrier goes down.** No exceptions.

This turns Act II from a chase into a war. The asset changes hands four or five times. **The player who found it on floor two is almost never the one who reaches the roof.**

It also converts Cling, Snag, and the pull-down into *objective play* rather than spite. You're not delaying out of malice — you're trying to make them drop the thing.

- Voluntary drop: 1 second
- Pick up: 1.5 seconds, vulnerable
- Cannot be carried while carrying a downed teammate
- **Cannot climb a ladder holding it** — must be thrown up first, to a teammate or a ledge

## 3.6 — The trade window

Two crews, one half each, both stuck. **They can actually trade** — a drop verb, proximity voice, **no enforcement of any kind.**

A negotiation between crews who all know:
- Neither gets out without the other
- The bird has four seats
- **Six people are standing in this room**

**Even a successful trade solves nothing.** It moves the fight from "who has the halves" to "who gets on the bird." The trade is honest and the aftermath is not, and everyone understands that while they're doing it.

Costs almost nothing to build. Produces a moment nothing else on the market does.

## 3.7 — Win, loss, and the clock

**Win condition: be on the bird.** No score, no points, no partial credit.

- **The roof door opens only when both halves reach it.**
- **Four seats.** Boarding is 3 seconds, interruptible. **You cannot board while clung to.**
- **Hard departure at dawn** regardless of occupancy.
- **If the door never opens, everybody loses.** Rare, brutal, and completely possible — it's what makes the trade window tense rather than theoretical.

# PART IV — COMBAT SCHEMATICS

*Unchanged from v0.4 except where noted. All values at 60fps, tuning starting points.*

## 4.1 — Design intent

Combat should feel like **two tired people trying to hurt each other in a room full of furniture.**

1. **Nothing is free.** Every action spends stamina, including defence.
2. **Everything commits.** Recovery frames are long. No cancelling.
3. **No invincibility frames exist anywhere.** You are always hittable.
4. **A third person changes everything.**
5. **The room is the weapon.**
6. **Every state has a verb.**

## 4.2 — Tracked states

| State | Range | What it is | Recovery |
|---|---|---|---|
| **STAMINA** | 0–100 | The currency | 14/sec after 1.8s delay |
| **BALANCE** | 0–100 | Posture. Breaks → stagger or knockdown | ~40/sec |
| **COMPOSURE** | 0–100 | Body damage | **None.** Items only. |
| **RATTLED** | 0–100 | Head trauma | **~1/sec.** Persists across floors. |
| **ADRENALINE** | 0–100 | Derived from duration and damage taken | Falls only out of combat |

## 4.3 — The stamina economy

| Action | Cost |
|---|---|
| Light strike | 8 |
| Heavy strike | 18 |
| Weak grapple | 12 |
| Strong grapple | 30 |
| **Environmental throw** | **55** |
| **Assisted throw** | **40 each ×2** |
| Grapple reversal | 30 |
| Slip | 12 |
| Cover (per hit) | 10 |
| Shove | 15 |
| Tie-up | 8/sec |
| Snag | 15 |
| Cling | 8/sec, escalating |
| Stomp-off (teammate / self) | 12 / 15 |
| Search a container | 5 |
| **Throw an object** | **18–30 by weight** |
| Sprint | 10/sec |
| Climb | 6/sec |
| Pull-down | 20 |
| Carry teammate | 4/sec |
| Force a door | 20/sec |
| Break debris (heavy) | 22/swing |
| Set a trap | 8–12 |

### Exhausted (0 stamina)

Not locked out. Damage **−50%** · reversals **unavailable** · guaranteed stagger on any hit · speed **−25%**, no sprint · **climb 45%** · **breathing audible** · **valid target for environmental throws.** Clears at 25.

## 4.4 — Adrenaline: the wave, mechanized

Grounded in real physiology: fine motor control degrades from ~115 BPM, complex motor sequences by ~145, cognition past ~175. Gross motor — swinging, shoving, tackling, grabbing — survives far longer.

| Level | State | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 0–30 | **Composed** | Full precision, full reversal windows |
| 30–60 | **Hot** | Reversal window −25%. Accuracy cone widens. |
| 60–85 | **Flooded** | Reversals unavailable. Weak grapples fail 20%. **Grip may slip.** |
| 85–100 | **Gone** | Gross motor only. Peripheral desaturates. Audio muffles. |

**This is the untrained-fighter system.** Early exchanges are clean; two minutes in, both players are swinging wild and throwing each other into furniture *because it's all the game allows.* The wave emerges without a scripted beat.

### Second Wind — Adrenaline's only upside

**Once per fight**, when genuinely in trouble: Composure below 30%, or recovering from a knockdown while outnumbered.

**+35 stamina instantly, 6 seconds at −20% costs.** Screen edges pulse, heartbeat in the mix, colour drains.

- Once per fight, resets after 20s out of combat
- **Costs +25 Adrenaline** — you bought stamina with precision
- **Unavailable while Concussed**

**Never refund stamina for *dealing* a knockdown.** That snowballs and destroys the recovery window.

## 4.5 — Strikes

| | Startup | Active | Recovery | Composure | Balance | Stamina |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Light | 6f | 3f | 12f | 8 | 15 | 8 |
| Heavy | 14f | 4f | 22f | 22 | 45 | 18 |
| Weapon light | 8f | 3f | 14f | 14 | 25 | 10 |
| Weapon heavy | 18f | 5f | 28f | 34 | 70 | 22 |

**No combos** · **heavies break Balance** → 18f stagger → grapple window · **weapon heavies break guard** → 24f stagger · strikes are **wide and looping** by animation.

## 4.6 — Defence (no dodge roll)

**SLIP — 12, ~6f window.** Lateral weight shift. Beats strikes only; a grab catches you mid-slip. Unavailable when Flooded. *The skill-expression move.*

**COVER — 10/hit.** Absorbs ~70%, protects the head. **You can't see, and you can be grabbed.**

**SHOVE — 15.** 2m of space, breaks a clinch, hits Balance. **Free against an exhausted opponent.**

**TIE-UP — 8/sec.** Both draining; higher remaining stamina wins the break.

**GIVE GROUND — free.** Backpedal, can't attack, slower than sprinting.

## 4.7 — The grapple system

Weak grapples **position.** Strong grapples **pay off.**

### Weak grapple — 12, ~0.8s

Neutral: knee to the gut · **Forward: collar drag — moves target 2–3m in your facing direction** · Back: shove-off · Side: arm wrench

**The collar drag is the most important move in the game.** It's how you walk someone toward the glass. The fight has a **spatial objective.**

### Strong grapple — 30, 2–3s, third-party vulnerable

Neutral: dump · Forward: overhead throw · Back: rear choke takedown · Side: hip toss

### The three tiers

| Tier | Cost | Requires | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Slam** — open floor | 30 | Nothing | Surface-dependent |
| **Environmental** — table, glass, cubicle, rack | **55** | Target **staggered or exhausted** | Massive impact, prop destroyed |
| **Assisted** — reinforced glass, railing, stairwell | **40 each ×2** | Two teammates in range, both opt in | Often instant Down |

**55 is more than half a bar** and more than a Heavy's pool tolerates. **The staggered-or-exhausted gate** means you can't open with your best move — and because exhaustion is audible, breathing tells you it's available.

**Fights get more spectacular as they get more desperate.**

### Assisted throws — contributed, not pooled

40 from *each* participant's own bar, both opt in, **nearby teammates' stamina visible** as a nameplate ring. Never pool.

### Reversals

8f window (Technician 12f) · 30 stamina · unavailable when Exhausted or Flooded · roles swap on success · **holding anything disables reversals.**

### Third-party interruption

| Intervention | Requirement | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Grapple break** | Light strike on either participant | Cancels. Attacker staggered 18f. |
| **Steal** | Heavy strike on the attacker | Third player takes over mid-move |
| **Pile-on** | 2+ simultaneous strikes | Both participants staggered |

### Vocabulary rules — hard constraints

- **No move names anywhere in the UI.** Nobody in the fiction knows what to call what they just did.
- **No held poses.** Everything up and down inside three seconds.
- **Low arcs**, below chest level except environmental throws.
- **Target the head, the joints, or the floor.**
- **No top-rope equivalents.**
- **If it looks practiced, it's wrong.** (See untrained body language, Part II.)

## 4.8 — Impact resolution

### Location
| Zone | Composure | Balance | Rattled |
|---|---|---|---|
| Head | Low | High | **High** |
| Jaw / temple | Low | **Very high** | **Very high** |
| Torso | **High** | Medium | None |
| Limb | Low | Low | Accumulates toward Broken |

### Awareness
Facing + braced **0.7×** · facing unbraced **1.0×** · peripheral **1.4×** · **blindside 2.2× with doubled Rattled**

### Landing surface
| Surface | Damage | Balance | Rattled | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Carpet / body | 0.6× | Low | Low | Office floors are merciful |
| Concrete | 1.3× | High | **High** | Garage, stairwells |
| Desk / table | 1.5× | High | Med | **Breaks** |
| Glass partition | 1.4× | Very high | Med | Breaks. **Bleeding.** |
| **Stair flight** | **1.8×** | Extreme | **High** | Tumbles |
| Railing / edge | 1.2× | Extreme | Low | Risk of going over |
| Server rack | 1.6× | High | Med | Collapses on target |
| Water (flood) | 0.4× | Med | None | See hazards |

### Landing orientation
Feet **0.5×** · side **1.0×** · back **1.3×** · **head/neck first 2.0× with heavy Rattled.** Rolled from exit velocity and remaining Balance.

### Force
Attacker archetype × weapon × impact velocity.

### Grip and the ground
**Weapons and the asset can be dropped** — high Adrenaline plus an arm hit gives a fumble chance.
**Knocked-down players are prone ~2s.** Standing over a prone opponent unlocks a stomp and a grounded grapple. No full ground game.

## 4.9 — Compromised: the states you can act from

| Action | Cost | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Snag** | 15 | Grab a leg from prone/kneeling. Target Balance −60. |
| **Cling** | 8/sec, escalating | Target 40% speed, no sprint, no climb, **cannot board the bird.** |
| **Drag down** | 22 | Both prone. |
| **Post up** | 10 | Grab furniture to stand faster. Recovery −30%. |

**Snag:** sprinting targets take it worst. **You cannot sprint past a downed enemy.** Downed players become terrain.

**Cling:** breaking it costs a shove (15) or a heavy strike — stamina *they were trying to save by leaving.* If they're gassed, they can't shake you. **Clothing tears** after ~6s. **Escalating cost** on re-Cling (8 → 12 → 18/sec).

**Stomp-off — 12, 0.5s, hits Balance not Composure.** Removing an obstruction, not attacking. Only breaks the Cling. **Adds Rattled, not damage.** **Self-stomp-off costs 15 and is slower — the cheap fix requires a teammate.**

**What one clinger costs a crew of three:** one held at 40%, one tasked with kicking, one watching the room. A dying man takes two-thirds of a crew off the board.

## 4.10 — The Rattled ladder

| Rattled | State | Presentation | Can do |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–25 | Fine | — | Everything |
| 25–50 | **Shaken** | Camera drifts, audio dulls, Adrenaline +15 | Everything, worse |
| 50–75 | **Dazed** | **Drops to a knee ~1.5s.** Blur, sway, input lag. | **Snag, Cling, Post up.** No strikes. |
| 75–95 | **Concussed** | Heavy sway, desaturation, ringing. No sprint, climb, reversals, or Second Wind. | **Cling only** |
| 95+ | **Down** | — | **Cling only, paid in Composure** |

**Recovers ~1/sec, does not reset between floors.**

### Worked example — the desk lamp

```
Location: head            →  Rattled +38, Composure −14, Balance −55
Awareness: peripheral     →  ×1.4
Lamp shatters             →  Bleeding (2/sec, 12s)
Total Rattled: 53         →  DAZED
```

**B** drops to a knee for 1.5s. He can get up. He is not okay. **And from that knee he can Snag or Cling.**

**A** sits at 31 stamina, Adrenaline 64 — **Flooded**. Reversals gone. **B can hear him breathing**, and so can anyone in the next room.

Push for the finish on empty, or back off and give B the same chance? **That is the fight.**

## 4.11 — Soft lock-on and camera

Auto-facing within **6m**, right-stick flick swaps targets, releases beyond **8m**. **2+ opponents → camera pulls back, FOV widens.** *The camera is the warning system.*

**Non-negotiable level rules:** minimum room **8m × 8m** · minimum doorway **3m** · minimum ceiling **4m** · wall dither · fight pull-back with wall-collision push-in.

## 4.12 — Friendly fire

**Always on. Never a toggle.** Without it, teamfights collapse into mashing and the 3-body door push carries no tension.

**Two rules make it survivable:**

1. **Environmental damage has no owner.** If your teammate throws someone into a pillar and it falls on you, that's the building. Only direct swings, throws, and stomps register as teammate-caused.
2. **Cap the punishment, not the damage.** Full FF Composure damage, **hard cap on Rattled from a teammate.** You can knock your buddy down. You can't accidentally concuss him out on floor two.

## 4.13 — Two hands

*New in v0.5. One clean rule that replaces a dozen exceptions.*

| Hand state | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Both free** | Full grapples, reversals, climbing, carrying |
| **One occupied** | Weapon attacks. **No reversals.** Climb at 70%. |
| **Both occupied** | No grapples, no reversals, **no climbing at all** |

Weapon = one hand. Asset = one hand. **Heavy weapon (sledge, fire axe) = both.** Carrying a teammate = both.

So a Heavy hauling a sledge and the asset **cannot climb a ladder.** Throw something up, hand it off, or drop it.

## 4.14 — Throwing

*New in v0.5. The only ranged option in the game.*

**Distance = archetype strength × object weight × stamina committed.** Hold to charge, release. Real arc, real gravity, no hitscan.

| Archetype | Light (bottle, stapler) | Medium (chair, monitor) | Heavy (extinguisher, drawer) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Heavy** | 14m | **9m** | **6m** |
| **Bruiser** | 12m | 6m | 3m |
| **Scrapper** | 11m | 5m | 2m |
| **Agile** | 10m | **3m** | **cannot lift** |
| **Technician** | 11m | 5m | 2.5m |
| **Medic** | 10m | 4m | 2m |

**Costs and consequences:**
- 18–30 stamina by weight
- **Long, readable windup.** Committed and visible.
- **You just disarmed yourself.**
- Impact resolves through the full model. **Blindside throws are devastating.**
- **Objects that fall short become floor clutter** — a Balance hazard. Your failed throw is still a small trap.

**The Heavy is the only real thrower.** Everyone else throws to interrupt — a bottle to break a climb, an extinguisher to break a grapple.

### The comedy is load-bearing

An Agile hurls a chair three yards and it clatters pathetically at her feet. **That's a character beat, not a bug.**

This game is grim, tired, and mean. **The physical comedy of failure is what makes it bearable and what makes it clippable** — the chair that doesn't reach, the throw that hits your own teammate, the oil you slipped in yourself. Real fights are undignified. Let them be.

## 4.15 — Injury system

**DOWNED** — Crawl at 1.2 m/s. Bleeding out **90s.** Revive 6s (Medic 3s). Carryable. **Can Cling, paid in Composure.** **Executable** — 4s, uninterruptible, fully exposed. **Drops the asset.**

**BROKEN** — Revived. Roll one:

| Injury | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Leg** | No sprint, **no climb**, no vault. 60% speed. |
| **Arm** | No heavy attacks, no grapples, no carrying, **no asset.** |
| **Ribs** | Stamina pool → 60. Regen −40%. |

Persists unless a **Medic stabilizes** (12s, splint).

**A broken player is a support unit, not a passenger.** Brace a door, hold a switch, count as a body, anchor a ladder base, guard a dropped asset.

**OUT** — Bled out or executed. Spectate. No respawn.

# PART V — THE BUILDING AS ANTAGONIST

## 5.1 — No NPCs

Creatures would double the project. **The building is the PvE.**

## 5.2 — The governing rule

> **Hazards don't pick sides. They resolve on everyone in the volume.**

Every hazard is a **tactical object**, not an obstacle. You don't avoid the flooded room — you fight *toward* it and time the exposure.

## 5.3 — Hazard catalogue

| Hazard | Behaviour | Tactical use |
|---|---|---|
| **Live line in standing water** | Arcs on a telegraphed cycle | Fight in the doorway, shove them in on the arc |
| **Falling pillar / ceiling** | Groaning audio, ~2s warning | Bait them under it |
| **Gas leak + spark** | Ignites on weapon impact or fire | A weapon-heavy in the wrong room kills you both |
| **Collapsing floor** | Fails under 2+ bodies | **The 3-body door push becomes a gamble** |
| **Steam pipe** | Bursts on impact, blinds in a cone | Aim your throws |
| **Elevator counterweight** | Drops if the cable is struck | Route trap |
| **Blown window + wind** | Pull force at the edge | Environmental throws become lethal |

## 5.4 — Scheduled escalation

**The disaster escalates on a clock the whole building can hear.** A structural groan, thirty seconds, then a section goes.

Everyone hears it. Everyone knows roughly where. **Three crews relocating at once, into each other.**

## 5.5 — Improvised traps

**Traps delay and reveal. They never damage.** Nothing kills. Everything costs the pursuer time or announces their position — perfect for a game where delay is the win condition, and it prevents a griefing meta.

| Trap | Cost | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Wedge a chair in a doorway** | 3s, 10 stam | 3s to clear, or one heavy swing |
| **Dump oil / spill a cooler** | Instant, 8 stam | Balance −40 on entry. Sprinting = fall. **Combos with the live wire.** |
| **Cut the lights** | 5s, 12 stam | Floor goes dark — including for you |
| **Jam an elevator** | 4s, 10 stam | Route closed until pried |
| **Discharge an extinguisher** | Instant | Blinding cloud, ~4s |
| **Scatter debris on stairs** | 3s, 8 stam | Noisy to cross — you hear them coming |

All diegetic office objects. Nothing purpose-built.

## 5.6 — Disaster variants

**🔥 FIRE** — Spreads on a timer, routes close dynamically, smoke cuts sightlines to ~8m. *The chase.*
**🌍 EARTHQUAKE** — Tremors, debris bait-able onto opponents, **routes open and close mid-match.** *The chaos.*
**🌀 TORNADO** — Windows blow out, exterior routes gain pull force, flying debris. *The vertical.*
**🌊 FLOOD** — Water rises on a hard timer, lower floors cease to exist. **Maximum synergy with live wires.** *The race.*

# PART VI — CLIMBING

## 6.1 — Climbing is a vulnerable state

Weapon sheathed · **no defence at all** · no attacks · 6 stamina/sec · camera locked.

**You cannot carry the asset up a ladder. It must be thrown up first.**

## 6.2 — Climb speed is your condition

| Condition | Speed |
|---|---|
| Healthy | 100% |
| Composure < 50% | 80% |
| **Exhausted** | **45%** |
| **Dazed** | **60%, with sway** |
| **Concussed** | **35%, periodic re-grip** |
| **Broken leg** | **Cannot climb** |
| Carrying a teammate | 50%, defenceless for both |

**This justifies Rattled persisting.** The lamp on floor 3 is why you're still on the ladder when they catch up on floor 7.

## 6.3 — The pull-down

**20 stamina, 0.6s.** Fall damage resolves through the full impact model.

| Height | Result |
|---|---|
| 1–3m | Prone, Balance gone, ~1.5s |
| 3–6m | Heavy Composure and Rattled, prone ~3s |
| **6m+** | **Likely instant Down — and the asset drops** |

**The higher they've climbed, the more it costs them to be caught.**

## 6.4 — The recovery window

```
KNOCKDOWN
    │
    ├── Downed: 2.0–4.5s to recover
    │
    └── Standing player chooses:
        ├── CLIMB   → ~4s to clear. Tight race.
        ├── STOMP   → +1.2s to their recovery, 12 stamina
        └── FINISH  → 4s, uninterruptible, guarantees the route
```

**The real question isn't "climb or stomp." It's "am I in good enough shape to make the climb, or must I spend time I don't have making sure he can't follow?"** A player who won badly can't take the fast option.

**Diminishing returns on stomps.** **Recovery is faster if the standing player is climbing** — the rubber band that makes the catch feel possible rather than scripted.

## 6.5 — Team climbing

**Single-file.** A crew of three takes 12+ seconds and is strung out the whole time.

**The anchor** — two climb, one holds the base. **A broken-leg player is the perfect anchor.**

**BRACE** — 1 teammate, +25% climb speed for everyone. The bracer can't fight.

**PULL UP** — 2 stamina/sec from the climber above. The thing you do for someone you refuse to leave.

## 6.6 — Contested climbs

Climbers **can be pulled down by anyone below, including a climber below them on the same ladder.** Players at the top can **stomp down** (15 stamina). **Nobody on a ladder can defend.**

## 6.7 — The match beat

> **search → hold or take → the alert → fight → knockdown → the window → the climb → someone gets caught → fight again**

The rooftop is consistent with everything below: the 3-second interruptible board is the last pull-down, at the top, with four seats.

# PART VII — ARCHETYPES

Six hand-tuned bodies. **No sliders.** Chosen in the lobby.

| | Composure | Stamina | Regen | Speed | Dmg | Special |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **HEAVY** | 130 | 70 | 9/s | 3.0 | ×1.35 | **2 bodies** for pushes. Breaks debris solo. Only real thrower. Climbs slowly. |
| **BRUISER** | 100 | 100 | 14/s | 3.4 | ×1.00 | Baseline |
| **SCRAPPER** | 75 | 130 | 20/s | 4.0 | ×0.80 | Weak grapples cost 6. No debris. |
| **AGILE** | 70 | 120 | 22/s | 4.2 | ×0.70 | **Squeeze-through.** Fastest climber. Searches 30% faster. |
| **TECHNICIAN** | 95 | 100 | 14/s | 3.4 | ×0.90 | **12f** reversal window. Reversals cost 20. |
| **MEDIC** | 85 | 110 | 16/s | 3.6 | ×0.70 | Revive 3s. Carry at 90%. Stabilizes Broken. |

## The Heavy / Agile axis

**Two different answers to the same blocked door.**

**HEAVY is force.** He *is* two bodies. He breaks debris solo. He opens routes by destroying them.

**AGILE is bypass.** **Squeeze-through** — transoms above doors, vent gaps, gaps in collapsed debris, broken windows. **Openings only she fits through.** She doesn't force the door, she goes around it and opens it from the other side. Her cost: alone on the far side, 70 Composure, no ability to fight whoever's waiting.

> *We have no Heavy and no Agile. Every jammed door needs all three of us — and if one goes down, we're locked out of half the building.*

A lobby decision you feel on floor six.

# PART VIII — TEAM SYSTEMS

## 8.1 — Headcount verbs

**Headcount gates cannot be solved by skill.**

| Task | Bodies |
|---|---|
| Force a jammed door | **3** (Heavy = 2, or **Agile bypasses**) |
| Brace a door shut | 1 (2 = 2× duration) |
| Boost to a high ledge | **2** |
| Clear heavy debris | **2**, or 1 with rare weapon, **or Agile squeezes** |
| Dual switch | **2**, separated |
| Carry downed teammate | 1, 60% speed, no weapon |
| Freight elevator | **2** |
| Assisted throw | **2** |
| Brace a ladder | **1** |
| Anchor a route | **1** |
| Stomp-off a clinger | **1** |
| Throw the asset up a shaft | **2** |

## 8.2 — Leave or carry

**Carry:** 60% speed, no weapon, no asset, 4/sec drain, 50% climb, nearly defenceless.

**Leave:** full capability. 90 seconds. You may be locked out of a route. They may be executed. **They may also Cling to whoever passes, or guard a dropped asset.**

**Extraction payment scales hard with crew size** — or randoms abandon each other and the best mechanic never fires.

## 8.3 — The 1v3 target

**Against the solo player:** blindside at 2.2× means someone is always behind you. A 1v3 in an open room is death, and should be.

**For them:** Adrenaline caps everyone's skill late. Corridors mean they can't all reach you. Their strong grapples are third-party vulnerable and yours aren't. Environmental throws don't care about numbers. Second Wind triggers on being outnumbered.

**The solo player's real weapon is geometry** — fight where only one fits.

**Balance lever: hunting must cost more than holding.** Three sprinting players burn 10/sec each; a defender in a doorway regenerates.

> **BALANCE TARGET: a 1v3 win in roughly 2–4% of eligible situations.**

Below 2%, doorways are too weak and the story never happens. Above 4%, teams are weak and the headcount design stops mattering. **Protect the rarity as carefully as the possibility.**

# PART IX — THE BUILDING

## 9.1 — Structure

8–10 floors, 3–4 rooms each, ~40m × 40m. Breach entry at flooded lower levels → rooftop helipad. **~90-second soft timer per floor in Act II.** Act I is untimed — the alert starts the clock.

## 9.2 — Floor themes (mandatory content)

| Floor | Props | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **Parking garage** | Cars, pillars, concrete, standing water | Brutal. Hard surfaces, high Rattled. |
| **Lobby** | Glass, planters, reception desk | Open, glass everywhere |
| **Office** | **Cubicles, glass partitions, desks, copiers** | **The fan favourite.** Every partition is a clip. |
| **Server floor** | Racks, cable trays, cage doors | Tight, collapsing shelving |
| **Residential** | Furniture, balconies, sliding doors | Soft floors — fights last longer |
| **Mechanical** | Pipes, catwalks, railings | Vertical. Railings mean falls. |
| **Roof** | Nothing but edges | Pure. No props, all consequence. |

**Budget: 6–8 prop types with paired throw animations.**

## 9.3 — Route randomization

All routes authored and present: main stairwell, elevator shaft climb, freight elevator (2 people), fire escape, service duct (single file, no weapons), ceiling breach, **transom/vent (Agile only).**

Server rolls **2 of 4–6** passable.

| Blocker | Breakable? | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Debris pile | **Yes** | 2 bodies, 1 with rare weapon, **or Agile** |
| Jammed door | **Yes** | 3 bodies, **or Agile opens from the far side** |
| Fire wall | Yes, extinguisher | Consumable |
| Structural gap | No | Boost (2 bodies) or reroute |
| Flooded stair | No | Hard block |

## 9.4 — The rooftop

**No props — only edges.** Storm, rotor wash, red strobes.

- **The door opens only when both halves arrive**
- **Boarding: 3 seconds**, interruptible, **cannot board while clung to**
- **Four seats.** Then it lifts.
- **Hard departure at dawn**

# PART XI — THE META: DEBT

*New in v0.5. Replaces cosmetics-only progression.*

## 11.1 — The problem this solves

Extraction dopamine is **loss aversion**, not acquisition. But this game can't do gear-as-power — six readable archetypes with hand-tuned stats is the foundation of the combat, and persistent gear stats would wreck it within a month.

**So what can you lose that isn't power? People. And time.**

## 11.2 — The debt number

**You have a debt. It goes down when you extract and up when you don't.**

| Outcome | Effect |
|---|---|
| Extract with an asset | Big paydown |
| Extract without it | Small paydown |
| Fail to extract | No paydown. Interest accrues. |
| **Get left behind** | **Debt transfers to your next character** |
| Die owing | Someone else in your roster inherits it |

**The loop isn't "get stronger." It's "get out from under it."**

**And you can never clear it entirely.** Get close and a new job appears. That's not a treadmill dressed up — it's the actual theme, and every player will recognize it.

## 11.3 — The crew roster

You don't pick "Heavy." You pick **Reyes**, who is a Heavy, who has done nine jobs, who has a bad shoulder from the last one, and who is two jobs from being free.

| Outcome | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **Extracted clean** | Paid. Available next job. |
| **Extracted injured** | Paid. **Out 2–3 jobs** recovering. |
| **Carried out downed** | Paid less. Out 4–5 jobs. |
| **Left behind** | **Gone. Permanently.** |
| **Executed** | Gone, and you get nothing. |

**Your roster is finite. Rebuilding is slow.** New people come in green with no history. That's the punishment — not a stat penalty, just the absence of the person you'd gotten attached to.

This makes the leave-or-carry decision matter **between** matches, not just during one. *We're only two because we left Marchetti on floor six last week.*

## 11.4 — What debt unlocks

**Horizontal, never power:**

- **Lower debt → better contracts** → harder buildings, worse disasters, bigger paydowns. Your difficulty progression, player-chosen.
- **Reputation** → better crew members willing to work with you
- **Cosmetics that read as status** — a jacket that says you've survived nine of these
- **Archetype access** — start with three, unlock six
- **Starting item choice**
- **New buildings, eventually**

## 11.5 — The run report

Underrated. A short after-action screen: floors climbed, times the asset changed hands, who held the door, who got left, debt before and after.

**Shareable.** It's what makes a run a story rather than a score.

## 11.6 — Scope warning

This meta-layer is real work — roster persistence, recruitment, contracts, injury tracking, save systems. **None of it belongs before Phase 4.**

Ship the alpha with no progression at all and find out if the *match* is fun. If it is, this layer makes people grind. If it isn't, no economy saves it.

# PART XII — PRODUCTION

## 12.1 — Risk register

| Risk | Severity | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| **Networked paired grapples** | **Critical** | Prototype month 3. **Project go/no-go.** |
| **Networked Cling/Snag** | **High** | Same problem class. Prototype alongside. |
| **Act I being boring** | **High** | Audible searching, costly searching, hold-or-take, proximity voice. **Test in Phase 3.** |
| Third-person camera indoors | High | 8m rooms, 3m doors, dither, pull-back |
| Randoms abandoning teammates | High | Payment scales with crew size. Design for premades. |
| **Crew identification in the dark** | **High** | Crew colour on one loud element. **Decide before any character art.** |
| **Animation packs reading as trained** | **Medium** | Hard filtering criterion. Prefer MMA/judo over wrestling/martial arts. |
| Camping an unclaimed asset | Medium | Dead air timer |
| Friendly fire griefing | Medium | Environmental damage unowned, Rattled cap |
| Cling lockdown | Medium | Escalating cost, clothing tear, stomp-off |
| 9–12 concurrent to start | Medium | Bot fill. FFA as lower-headcount entry. |

## 12.2 — Roadmap

**Phase 1 — Local combat (months 1–3)** — Grey capsules, one room, offline. All five states, strikes, five defence options, weak/strong grapple, one prop. Compromised verbs. Heavy and Scrapper. Gauntlet harness.
**Gate:** *Does a Heavy vs. Scrapper fight feel tense? Does the table throw feel earned?*

**Phase 2 — Networked 1v1 (months 4–6)** — Two clients, one paired grapple, one Cling, real latency.
**Gate:** *Does a paired throw hold up at 80ms?* **Project go/no-go.**

**Phase 3 — 3v3v3 on two floors (months 7–13)** — Both acts. Search, hold-or-take, the alert, one asset. Revive/carry, one 3-body door, one climbable route, assisted throws, stomp-off, office prop set, friendly fire, one hazard.
**Gate:** *Does Act I hold attention? Does the alert land? Does leave-or-carry hurt?*

**Phase 4 — Full building alpha (months 14–26)** — 8–10 floors, six archetypes, two assets, the trade window, one disaster, hazards, traps, rooftop, FFA + 3v3v3. Debt meta.

**Phase 5 — Post-alpha** — Additional disasters, 2v2v2 and 4v4v4, second building.

## 12.3 — Scope discipline

**One building, one fiction, four disasters, for the entire first release.**

Variety comes from the disaster roll, the asset spawn, and route randomization — already dozens of distinct matches out of one map, and it's why this stays possible for one person.
